Two complementary scoring models

Both are educational. Neither guarantees real-world safety or compromise.

A · PRIVACY ASSESSMENT SCORE

The questionnaire-driven model. Ten categories (profile, personal information, location, content, connections, tagging, account security, third-party apps, social engineering, digital footprint) are each scored 0–100 and combined with configurable weights (15/15/15/10/10/10/10/5/5/5 %) into one 0–100 score. It measures behaviour and configuration.

B · PRIVACY EXPOSURE RUBRIC

The exposure-dimension model. Eight dimensions contribute capped points — PII 25 · Geolocation/EXIF 20 · Children/Minors 15 · Workplace/School 10 · Weak Privacy Settings 10 · Linkage 10 · Handle Reuse 5 · Trackers 5 — and the final score is min(100, sum). It measures specific exposure dimensions.

Not equivalent. The two models use different mathematics (weighted mean vs capped additive points) and will normally produce different numbers for the same person. They are complementary views, and both are educational assumptions that should be validated before professional use.

The 0–100 privacy risk model

Higher score = higher assessed exposure/risk.

LOW · 0–20

Strong privacy configuration with few exposed attributes.

MODERATE · 21–40

Reasonable baseline, several settings still worth tightening.

HIGH · 41–70

Multiple exposure points across categories; act on the priority list.

CRITICAL · 71–100

Extensive public exposure combined with weak account controls.

Educational framework. This is an educational risk framework and does not guarantee that an account will or will not be compromised. The weights and thresholds are educational assumptions and should be validated before professional risk decisions.

👶 Children & minors in media

A dedicated exposure dimension worth up to 15 rubric points. The framework asks only whether child-related media, posts and school information are publicly visible — it never asks for a child's name, school, address or any other personal detail, and it never identifies anyone.

🔗 Linkage, handle reuse & trackers

Link-in-bio aggregators, publicly linked documents and repositories, re-used usernames and third-party trackers on linked sites are assessed from self-reported answers or voluntarily supplied synthetic export files. No account is searched, enumerated or crawled.

Privacy vs Security

Privacy is about controlling who can see what about you — audience settings, visibility of contact details, location sharing and digital footprint.

Security is about protecting the account itself — MFA, login alerts, unique passwords, session review and third-party app permissions.

This framework scores both, because oversharing plus weak account controls is what actually turns exposure into an incident.

How oversharing increases social-engineering risk

Publicly visible details make a scam message feel personal and legitimate. Categories that most often get re-used in pretexting are:

EmployerCollege Travel plansFamily references InterestsPublic events

The defence is conceptual, not technical: reduce what is public, verify unusual requests through a second channel, and never share verification codes. This project deliberately contains no attack scripts or personalised pretext messages.

Privacy by Design inside this application

Each principle is implemented, not just documented.

Data Minimisation

Only scores, finding types and timestamps are stored. Raw answers are never persisted.

Purpose Limitation

Stored data is used only for your report and anonymised aggregate analytics.

Least Privilege

The API exposes read-only aggregates; admin-style actions require a configured key.

Privacy by Default

No accounts, no tracking, no cookies, no analytics scripts, no third-party CDNs.

Transparency

The scoring model, weights and thresholds are open and fully explainable.

User Control

DELETE /api/assessment/{id} erases an assessment on request.

Retention Limitation

Nothing that could identify you is retained — there is nothing to expire.

Secure Processing

Strict input validation, output escaping, security headers and rate limiting.

Ethical scope

This project is designed for defensive cybersecurity and privacy education. It uses synthetic or voluntarily provided assessment responses and does not scrape, track or profile real social-media users. It never asks for your actual phone number, e-mail address, postal address, birth date or password — only whether such information is publicly visible.
